## 1. Problem Context & Background

Urban Heat Islands (UHI) are microclimatic phenomena wherein urbanized metropolitan areas experience significantly elevated surface and atmospheric temperatures compared to peripheral rural environments. In dense cities, solar radiation is absorbed and retained by dark, low-albedo building materials (asphalt roads, tar roofs) and re-radiated into pedestrian canyons. The deficit of vegetative canopy suppresses natural evaporative cooling, while anthropogenic heat emissions from transportation and air conditioning further exacerbate ambient thermal stress.

Traditional municipal responses to urban heat face critical bottlenecks:
1. **Disjointed Data Silos:** Thermal satellite rasters, parcel cadastres, and demographic census tables live in separate formats without unified spatial risk indexes.
2. **Black-Box Scoring:** Decision-makers are often presented with opaque heat indices without mathematical decomposition into physical hazard vs demographic exposure.
3. **Lack of Actionable Intervention Modeling:** City planners lack tools to test counterfactual mitigation portfolios (e.g., cool roofs vs street canopy expansion) against specific spatial limits and municipal budgets.
4. **LLM Overreach & Hallucination:** Emerging tools attempt to generate urban policy via generative chatbots, introducing severe risks of fabricated thermal claims and unreliable capital cost projections.

THERMOS is purpose-built to solve these bottlenecks through an auditable, deterministic decision-intelligence architecture.

## 2. Product Vision & Value Proposition

**THERMOS** is an urban climate decision-intelligence workbench engineered to give municipal resilience teams, urban GIS analysts, and civil engineering departments a transparent computational engine for heat mitigation planning.

### Core Value Pillars:
* **Mathematical Verifiability:** Every heat anomaly, risk score, and cooling estimate is generated by explicit, reproducible mathematical formulas defined in [thermal_analytics.py](../backend/app/modules/heat/thermal_analytics.py), [risk_engine.py](../backend/app/modules/risk/risk_engine.py), and [scenario_engine.py](../backend/app/modules/simulation/scenario_engine.py).
* **Actionable Spatial Diagnostics:** Translates raw sensor/satellite observations into localized urban zones with explainable component attribution bars (Hazard, Exposure, Vulnerability).
* **Interactive Counterfactual Simulation:** Enables planners to allocate cooling interventions to available physical surfaces (roofs, street corridors, pavements, public spaces), immediately receiving simulated LST reductions, ambient 2m air temperature cooling deltas, and budget utilization tallies.
* **Auditable Provenance:** Every metric displays its data classification (`OBSERVED`, `DERIVED`, `ESTIMATED`, `SIMULATED`, `ASSUMED`), preventing false scientific certainty.

## 3. User Personas & Workflows

### Persona 1: Municipal Chief Resilience Officer (CRO)
* **Goal:** Prioritize capital improvement grants across city districts to protect vulnerable populations from heatwaves.
* **Workflow in THERMOS:**
  1. Opens city dashboard and inspects high-level summary tiles (Average Risk, Hotspot count, Population exposed).
  2. Filters the hotspot list by `CRITICAL` or `SEVERE` tiers.
  3. Inspects zone dossiers to identify whether primary drivers are canopy deficits or demographic concentrations.
  4. Formulates budget envelopes for priority wards.

### Persona 2: Urban GIS Specialist / Environmental Analyst
* **Goal:** Audit land surface temperature anomalies against zoning codes, building footprint ratios, and surface albedo.
* **Workflow in THERMOS:**
  1. Interacts with the MapLibre GL choropleth map, toggling between Light and Dark cartographic basemaps.
  2. Inspects polygon boundaries, impervious surface fractions, and Land Surface Temperature ($\text{LST}$) differentials ($\Delta T$).
  3. Extracts RFC 7946 GeoJSON payloads via REST endpoints (`/api/v1/zones/geojson`) for cross-referencing in municipal GIS software (QGIS, ArcGIS Pro).

### Persona 3: Civil Works & Urban Forestry Engineer
* **Goal:** Design site-specific cooling plans within physical parcel limits (roof areas, sidewalk widths) and capital expenditure caps.
* **Workflow in THERMOS:**
  1. Navigates to a specific high-risk zone (e.g., `ZONE-01` or `ZONE-02`).
  2. Opens the **Intervention Planning Workbench**.
  3. Reviews algorithmically recommended cooling solutions tailored to the zone's surface constraints.
  4. Adjusts deployment selections and budget ceiling in INR Lakhs (₹).
  5. Reviews real-time simulated surface cooling ($\Delta T_{\text{LST}}$), ambient cooling ($\Delta T_{\text{ambient}}$), capital expenditure, budget utilization, and population benefited.

## 5. Explicit System Boundaries & Non-Goals

To preserve scientific integrity, reliability, and computational performance, THERMOS adheres to explicit design non-goals:

1. **No Generative Mathematics:** Large Language Models are never permitted to generate temperatures, risks, costs, or area limits. All numbers originate from deterministic Python modules.
2. **No Unvalidated Physics Claims:** While the scenario engine employs physics-inspired empirical models (exponential saturation, synergy factors), all outputs are strictly flagged with `DataClassification.SIMULATED` and accompanied by the disclaimer: *"Modelled estimate under stated assumptions; not field-validated."*
3. **No 3D Computational Fluid Dynamics (CFD):** THERMOS is an urban-scale planning decision platform, not a microscale building aerodynamic simulator. Phase 1 models 2-meter ambient cooling via empirical transfer functions rather than solving Navier-Stokes equations.
4. **No Heavy Database Dependency for Evaluation:** The Phase 1 MVP is fully operational using an in-memory repository loaded from pre-validated JSON datasets ([sample_zones.json](../data/processed/sample_zones.json)), eliminating the barrier of local PostgreSQL/PostGIS setup during demonstrations.
5. **No Production Dependency on External Scan Folders:** The `Thermos_Friend_Frontend_Scan/` directory is strictly an external read-only audit artifact and UI design reference from competitive analysis; it is completely detached from the THERMOS production application and build pipeline.
