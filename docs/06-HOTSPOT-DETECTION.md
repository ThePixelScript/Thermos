## 1. Hotspot Qualification Logic

In urban resilience planning, an area may demand intervention due to either severe physical thermal radiation OR severe population vulnerability under moderate heat. 

THERMOS resolves this dual challenge in [backend/app/modules/heat/hotspot_detection.py](../backend/app/modules/heat/hotspot_detection.py) by employing a **dual-criterion qualification rule**:

$$\text{is\_hotspot} = (\text{CHRI} \ge 50.0) \lor (\Delta T \ge 3.0^\circ\text{C})$$

### Operational Justification:
* **The High-Exposure Case:** An informal settlement or dense market quarter might exhibit an anomaly of $\Delta T = +2.8^\circ\text{C}$ (below a pure thermal anomaly threshold), but because population density exceeds $35,000 / \text{km}^2$ and air conditioning prevalence is below $10\%$, its CHRI exceeds $70.0$. This zone qualifies immediately.
* **The High-Hazard Industrial Case:** An industrial logistics railyard or manufacturing park may have minimal resident population, but asphalt and metal roofs radiate an anomaly of $\Delta T = +10.5^\circ\text{C}$. This creates a strong micro-urban thermal bubble that advects heat into neighboring wards. This zone qualifies immediately under the thermal criterion.

## 2. Priority Classification Tiers

Zones that satisfy the base qualification criteria are classified into four deterministic operational priority tiers:

| Priority Tier | Trigger Conditions | Operational Directives |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| `CRITICAL_HOTSPOT` | $\text{CHRI} \ge 85.0 \;\lor\; \Delta T \ge 12.0^\circ\text{C}$ | Priority 1 emergency intervention; mobile cooling shelters, mandatory work breaks, fast-track cool roof grants. |
| `SEVERE_HOTSPOT` | $\text{CHRI} \ge 70.0 \;\lor\; \Delta T \ge 8.0^\circ\text{C}$ | Priority 2 capital funding; tree canopy expansion, municipal pavement retrofits. |
| `HIGH_HOTSPOT` | $\text{CHRI} \ge 50.0 \;\lor\; \Delta T \ge 4.0^\circ\text{C}$ | Priority 3 planning target; green infrastructure mandates for redevelopment permits. |
| `MODERATE_HOTSPOT` | All other qualifying zones | Priority 4 monitoring; routine maintenance and canopy preservation. |

## 3. Hotspot Ranking & Sorting Algorithm

When querying the city-wide hotspot registry via `/api/v1/hotspots` (implemented in [backend/app/api/hotspots.py](../backend/app/api/hotspots.py)), all qualifying zones are ranked using a multi-attribute lexicographical sort:

```python
# Ranking implementation in backend/app/data/repository.py and api/hotspots.py
qualifying_hotspots.sort(
    key=lambda h: (h.chri_score, h.thermal_anomaly_c),
    reverse=True
)

for rank, hotspot in enumerate(qualifying_hotspots, start=1):
    hotspot.priority_rank = rank
```

1. **Primary Sort Key:** Composite Heat Risk Index (`chri_score`) descending. High-risk, vulnerable populations receive precedence.
2. **Secondary Sort Key (Tiebreaker):** Land Surface Temperature anomaly (`thermal_anomaly_c`) descending.
3. **Priority Rank:** 1-indexed contiguous ranking ($1, 2, 3, \dots, N$) assigned after sorting.

## 4. Hotspot Detailed Dossier Generation

When a municipal planner selects a specific hotspot (`GET /api/v1/hotspots/{zone_id}`), the backend generates an enriched `HotspotDetail` object ([backend/app/schemas/hotspot.py](../backend/app/schemas/hotspot.py)):

* **Spatial Attributes:** Zone ID, name, area in $\text{km}^2$, geometric centroid coordinates for map fly-to navigation.
* **Thermal Metrics:** Surface temperature, baseline temperature, and localized thermal anomaly ($\Delta T$).
* **Dominant Driver Identification:** The single environmental or demographic metric contributing the highest percentage to the CHRI score (e.g. *"Low Albedo Surface Deficit (34.2%)"*).
* **Driver Breakdown:** Complete table of all 7 underlying drivers with raw values, normalized scores, weights, and percentage attributions.
* **Actionable Recommendations:** Top 3 site-tailored cooling interventions generated by the intervention recommender, with estimated unit costs and cooling potential.
* **Audit Lineage:** Data classification badges (`OBSERVED`, `DERIVED`, `SIMULATED`) and model assumptions.
